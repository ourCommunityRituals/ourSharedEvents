# Target-List Framework — Choosing Discovery Conversation #1-5

**For:** OSE-271 — first 5 bar-owner discovery conversations.
**Goal:** pick five venues whose answers, taken together, span the BEACHHEAD-ICP claim space.

If you talk to five copies of the same kind of bar, you'll get one signal repeated five times. **Span the space**, then run the discipline of confirming the patterns later with five more.

---

## What "spanning the space" means here

The BEACHHEAD-ICP claim is _medium-and-smaller adult venues running recurring sports leagues + trivia_. That ICP statement hides at least four meaningful dimensions of variation. Your five conversations should hit different points across each.

### Dimension 1 — Venue type

| Type                                | Likely characteristics                                                 | Representative example                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Sports bar**                      | TVs dominant, large bar, draws crowds for games + has slow nights      | A neighborhood spot with 8+ TVs that does trivia Tuesdays    |
| **Taproom / craft beer**            | Curated tap list, no TVs or few TVs, draws regulars, often has events  | A craft taproom that hosts a trivia night to fill Wednesdays |
| **Cocktail bar / neighborhood pub** | Smaller, food-anchored, draws regulars by name                         | A neighborhood pub with weekly trivia or a dart league       |
| **Brewery taproom**                 | Owns its own product, often community-oriented, hosts wide event range | A brewery taproom doing trivia + run club + open mic         |
| **Restaurant with bar**             | Primary identity is food, bar/event is secondary                       | A restaurant that runs a Thursday trivia for the bar crowd   |

Pick at least 3 distinct types in your first 5.

### Dimension 2 — Event maturity

| Maturity                | What it looks like                     | What it tests                                   |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **No recurring events** | Open mic happened once, never again    | Tests _why_ they don't run events               |
| **Tried-and-dropped**   | Trivia ran for 6 months, fizzled       | Tests the gap that killed the prior attempt     |
| **Currently running**   | Trivia + league + open mic, every week | Tests the operational pain at scale             |
| **Saturated runner**    | 4+ event series, well-oiled machine    | Tests pricing displacement and channel dynamics |

Try to hit 3 of the 4. Especially valuable: at least one _tried-and-dropped_ (they know the failure mode); at least one _currently running_ (the BEACHHEAD-ICP's literal target).

### Dimension 3 — Franchise vs. homegrown

| Pattern              | What it looks like                                                  | What it tests                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Franchise-driven** | Geeks Who Drink / Trivia Time / regional independent runs the event | Tests whether the franchise is the real buyer           |
| **Homegrown**        | Bartender or owner hosts trivia themselves                          | Tests whether the operational pain is bigger or smaller |
| **Hybrid**           | Franchise for trivia, homegrown for league night                    | Tests both patterns in the same venue                   |

At least one franchise-driven and at least one homegrown in your first 5.

### Dimension 4 — Neighborhood density

| Density                | Example (Chicago framing)                                           | What it tests                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Saturated corridor** | Lincoln Park, Wicker Park — 5+ bars on a 4-block stretch            | Tests whether competition heightens the value of being measured       |
| **Anchor corridor**    | Beverly, Bridgeport — your venue is the corridor's only or main bar | Tests whether being the only game in town changes the buyer's mindset |
| **Mixed / suburban**   | Edge-of-city, lower density, more drive-than-walk                   | Tests whether the corridor model breaks when foot-traffic is thin     |

Hit at least 2 of the 3.

## Suggested first-five selection (Chicago metro framing)

Adapt the neighborhoods to your own market. The shape is what matters.

| #   | Venue type                          | Maturity            | Franchise?       | Density            | Why this one                                                                                       |
| --- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Neighborhood sports bar             | Currently running   | Franchise-driven | Saturated corridor | The BEACHHEAD-ICP's literal target; your control case                                              |
| 2   | Craft taproom                       | Currently running   | Homegrown        | Saturated corridor | Tests whether the franchise channel matters or is incidental                                       |
| 3   | Brewery taproom                     | Saturated runner    | Hybrid           | Anchor corridor    | The "best case" — most events, most pain, most likely to articulate the gap                        |
| 4   | Neighborhood pub                    | Tried-and-dropped   | Homegrown        | Anchor corridor    | The "failure mode" — why doesn't it work for this kind of venue?                                   |
| 5   | Cocktail bar or restaurant-with-bar | No recurring events | N/A              | Mixed              | The "out-of-ICP" — does the platform's value translate to a venue that doesn't fit the hypothesis? |

If five conversations give you five wildly different stories, you don't have a beachhead; you have five buyer hypotheses. If five conversations give you four overlapping stories with one outlier, the four overlapping is your real ICP and the outlier is a useful contradiction.

## How to find them

### Sources, ranked by signal quality

1. **Walk-by during the day.** Notice which bars in your neighborhood are open at 2pm and have an owner / manager visible. Note the address. Come back at 2:30pm on a weekday — the slow window when management is usually around.

2. **Recurring-event calendars.** Search "trivia Tuesday + [neighborhood]" or check sites like Geeks Who Drink's venue locator. Bars that _advertise_ their event series are easier to start a conversation with than bars where the event is buried in the calendar.

3. **Local Instagram.** Search the neighborhood + "trivia night" or "league" hashtag. The bars actively posting are more likely to be willing to talk to a stranger.

4. **Cold-walk-in (lowest signal but highest learning).** Walk into 8 places in a Saturday afternoon. Maybe 2 will give you 10 minutes. Those 2 are your first conversations. The 6 that didn't engage tell you something about cold-walk-in as a channel.

5. **Snowball from conversation #1.** After your first conversation, the snowball question ("anyone else in your circle who runs events like yours?") usually produces 1-3 referrals. Use them.

### Sources to AVOID

- **Restaurant industry forums / FB groups.** People posting there are the saturated runners. They're not the median ICP; they're the outlier on the "saturated runner" axis. You'll get one good conversation but distorted signal.
- **Yelp Top 10 lists.** These are tourist-trap venues with high marketing budgets. Their answers about pricing and tooling won't translate to your real ICP.
- **Big chain bars.** Buffalo Wild Wings runs trivia, but the decision-maker isn't on premises and the platform decisions are made at HQ. Different sales motion entirely.

## What "good enough" looks like for the first 5

You're not running a survey. You're stress-testing a hypothesis. The acceptance criterion for the first batch is:

- 5 conversations completed, anonymized notes captured.
- At least 3 dimensions of variation (venue type, maturity, franchise pattern) represented across the 5.
- A clear pattern visible: either (a) 4+ conversations point the same direction on the central claims = beachhead validated, or (b) 3+ conversations contradict a core claim = stop and reassess.
- One sentence per conversation in the synthesis doc: _"Owner of a [type] in [neighborhood], [N] years in business, runs [event pattern], [key surprise or confirmation]."_

If you can't write that one-sentence summary from memory after the conversation, the conversation didn't land. Try again with conversation #6.

---

## A note on incentives

You may be tempted to compensate interviewees ($20 gift card, free drink, etc.). **Don't, in this first batch.** Compensation creates a small but real bias toward telling you what you want to hear. The interviewees who'll talk to you for free are the ones who genuinely have something to say about their business.

If a conversation goes long and they bought you a drink, leave a generous tip and a sincere thank-you. That's the etiquette, not a transaction.
