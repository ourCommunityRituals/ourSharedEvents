# Discovery Interview Guide — Bar / Taproom / Pub Owners

**Use this for:** OSE-271 — first 5 bar-owner discovery conversations.
**Target time per conversation:** 25–40 minutes.
**Mode:** in-person preferred (afternoon, before dinner rush); video acceptable for second choice.
**Audience for this doc:** the OSE founder going into the conversation.

---

## Before you walk in

**Goal in one sentence:** validate whether the BEACHHEAD-ICP hypothesis (medium-and-smaller adult venues running recurring sports leagues + trivia would pay $149/mo for a sponsor-grade corridor-density analytics product) is real, partially real, or wrong-shaped.

**You are not here to sell.** You are here to listen. Every minute you spend talking about OSE is a minute you didn't learn something about their business. Default ratio: 80% them talking, 20% you.

**Listening for, specifically:**

- Words _they_ use for the events (trivia night, league night, Thursday crowd, regulars, the bocce people). Borrow their language for the BEACHHEAD-ICP doc; it's worth more than your own.
- The _gap_ in their workflow they wish someone solved. Marketing? Headcount? Sponsorship? Calendar?
- Whether they currently capture attendee data (sign-in sheets, raffles, email lists). This is the architectural-moat reality test.
- How they currently make decisions about whether to keep running an event series.
- The names of any tools they already pay for — the displacement landscape.

**Don't lead the witness.** Bad: "Wouldn't you love a tool that gives you sponsor-grade analytics?" Good: "How do you know if a Thursday trivia night is working?"

---

## The conversation flow

The flow has five phases. Don't read the questions verbatim — use them as a backbone. Skip what's already been answered; chase what surprises you.

### Phase 1 — Open the door (3-5 min)

Get them comfortable. Establish that you're not selling.

- _"How long have you owned / managed this place?"_
- _"How's the night-of-week mix going for you these days?"_ → listens for whether they're frustrated, growing, plateauing, or worried
- _"Tell me what a slow Tuesday looks like vs. a packed Saturday."_

**Listening for:** are they an _operator_ (knows numbers cold) or an _owner_ (knows the regulars by name but not the math)? Your pitch language has to match.

### Phase 2 — How recurring events actually work here (8-12 min)

This is the meat. Most of your minutes go here.

- _"Do you run anything recurring — trivia, league nights, open mic?"_ → if no, gently probe whether they've tried in the past and what happened
- _"Walk me through how Thursday trivia (or whatever they named) actually runs. Who runs it? Who shows up?"_
- _"How do you know it's working?"_ → critical question. Are they measuring? By what? Bartender intuition? Drink revenue? Headcount?
- _"Who decides whether to keep it on the calendar?"_ → owner / GM / outside franchise / bar manager?
- _"Are you working with an outside trivia company, or is it homegrown?"_ → tests the franchise-channel hypothesis (OSE-267)
- _"What's the worst part of running a recurring event for you?"_ → the gap statement. Listen hard.
- _"What's the best part?"_ → tells you what they actually value

**Red flags to listen for:**

- They run zero recurring events and don't want to → wrong ICP, end early, thank them
- The trivia franchise _is_ the operator and the bar is hands-off → buyer is the franchise, not the bar (changes the sales motion)
- They already pay for a robust analytics tool they love → displacement is harder than the doc assumed

### Phase 3 — Sponsors and money (5-8 min)

The economic engine. Be direct.

- _"Do any local sponsors put money into your events? Insurance broker, the credit union, urgent care, real estate folks — anyone like that?"_
- If yes: _"How did that relationship start? What do they get for what they pay?"_
- If no: _"Have you ever thought about it? What stopped you?"_
- _"If a local insurance broker came in and said 'I'll give you $400 a month to be the sponsor on your trivia nights for a season,' what would you say?"_ → this is the SKU validation question; listen for the _first reaction_, not the considered answer
- _"What's your monthly SaaS or platform spend look like? Yelp Premium, OpenTable, Toast, anyone like that?"_ → pricing anchor reality
- _"What's the most useful thing any of those tools have actually delivered for you?"_

**Listening for:** is the sponsor relationship already a thing they want and can't get? Or is it a thing they've never thought about? Big difference for the pitch.

### Phase 4 — The data + privacy moment (5-7 min)

The architectural moat in plain language. Listen for whether it resonates or whether they care at all.

- _"Do you keep any kind of list of people who come to trivia? Sign-in sheet, raffle entries, anything?"_
- If yes: _"What do you use it for? Has anyone ever objected to giving you their info?"_
- If no: _"Why not? Or just never set up?"_
- _"If I told you there's a way to get a monthly report on your event nights — how many unique people showed up, whether they come back, how busy your spot is compared to the surrounding neighborhood — but you'd never see anyone's name or email. Would that be useful or pointless?"_ → the architectural-moat reality test, asked operationally
- _"Has any of your regulars ever asked about privacy when you tried to do something digital?"_

**Listening for:** does anonymity matter to _them_? It matters to _attendees_ by hypothesis — does the bar owner _care_ that it matters? If not, the moat is a feature they tolerate, not a feature they buy.

### Phase 5 — The wrap (3-5 min)

Hand back control. Open the door for follow-up.

- _"If I came back in three months with a working version of something I'm building — would you want to see it?"_
- _"Anything I should have asked that I didn't?"_ → always ask this. The answer is often the most valuable minute.
- _"Is there anyone else in your circle running events like yours who'd be a good person for me to talk to?"_ → snowball into conversation #6, #7, #8

**Don't make a promise you can't keep.** If you don't have a working SharedReport yet, don't say you'll show one. Say "I'll come back when I do."

## What you're testing, mapped to BEACHHEAD-ICP claims

| BEACHHEAD-ICP claim                   | Conversation evidence that supports it                        | Conversation evidence that contradicts it                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bars with recurring events are an ICP | They run 1+ recurring event series and want more              | They tried it and stopped, or never bothered                   |
| The buyer is the bar owner / GM       | The owner is the decision-maker on events                     | The franchise runs it; the bar is a venue, not a decider       |
| Sponsor wedge is real                 | They already work with 1+ local sponsor or have wanted to     | They've never thought about sponsors and have no interest      |
| $149/mo is a believable price         | Their current SaaS spend is in the $50-200/mo range           | Their current SaaS spend is $0 or they push back hard on price |
| Anonymity matters to attendees        | They've heard attendee pushback on data collection            | No one has ever objected to a sign-in sheet                    |
| Trivia franchises are a channel       | They work with a franchise that they like + that's grown them | They're solo / homegrown / hate the franchise                  |
| Corridor density is interesting       | They light up when you describe the monthly report            | They shrug and say "headcount is enough"                       |

## Red flags that should pause the BEACHHEAD-ICP positioning work

Per OSE-271's stop criterion: **if 3+ of 5 conversations contradict a core claim, pause and reassess.**

The three most consequential contradiction patterns to watch for:

1. **Nobody runs recurring events because they don't drive enough drink revenue.** If 3+ bars say "we tried trivia, didn't move the needle, dropped it" — the ICP is wrong-shaped. OSE needs a different beachhead vertical.

2. **The franchise is the buyer, not the bar.** If 3+ bars say "the trivia company handles all of that" — the sales motion is franchise-first, not bar-first. The product is the same; the GTM is different.

3. **Local sponsors don't exist at this scale.** If 3+ bars say "no insurance broker has ever offered to sponsor us" — the sponsor wedge is conceptual, not real. Either the bars don't know how to find sponsors (a product opportunity) or sponsors don't think bar events are worth their budget (an ICP problem).

If you hit any of these, **don't push through to interview #6.** Stop, write up the contradiction, and bring it to a strategy review before continuing.

## After the conversation

- Walk to your car / find a bench / sit down somewhere quiet. **Do not start another conversation immediately.**
- Open `INTERVIEW-TEMPLATE-2026-05-14.md` and fill it out from memory. The first 30 minutes after a conversation are when memory is sharpest; an hour later, you'll have lost half the texture.
- Anonymize at write time. Use venue type / neighborhood / role, never names or addresses.
- Note one thing that surprised you and one thing that confirmed a prior. The surprises are where the value lives.

---

## What to bring with you

- A notebook (paper, not phone — taking notes on a phone makes them think you're texting)
- One business card if you have one
- **Nothing else.** No deck, no demo, no leave-behind. You are not selling. The asymmetry of "I'm just trying to learn" is your strongest position.

If they ask "what are you building?" — keep it under 30 seconds: _"I'm working on a tool for bars that run recurring events like trivia. I'm trying to figure out if it solves a problem before I keep building. So I'm talking to people like you first."_ Then ask them another question.
