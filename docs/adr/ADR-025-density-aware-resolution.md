# ADR-025: Make published resolution depend on census population density, not on our own data

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-10). Two things it does not have yet: the owner's choice of mechanism, and the density thresholds from the external privacy reviewer. Nothing in it is built.
**Date:** 2026-11-10
**Deciders:** Owner (PO); the external privacy-engineering reviewer supplies the thresholds
**Ticket:** OSE-1944

## Context

The under-5 floor (AGENTS.md invariant 2) is a fixed count. The protection it gives depends on how many
people the counted person could be, and that number is set by density, not by the floor.

- A res-9 cell is about 0.105 km² (an edge of about 174 m). In Mount Greenwood, Chicago (about 5,000
  residents per km²), that is on the order of 500 people. Five scans there are anonymous.
- The same cell near Sawyer, Michigan, may hold two or three households. Five scans in one hour there
  can be one family and a neighbour.
- The hourly time grain (owner decision 2026-10-01, OSE-1460) narrows the set of possible people
  further.

The geography rules are in OCR-docs ADR-011 (Accepted). It says res-9 is for physical places, the
corridor is the res-7 cell, and every export is res-7 or coarser. That ADR explicitly "does not set
export time grain or K-floors", and nothing else in the ADRs, the invariant register or the board
covers density.

**Where a count is published at res-9 today** (from the code on `main` at `4ab91e48`):

| Surface                         | Code                                           | What is published                                                    |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SharedReport poster attribution | `src/lib/shared-report-builder.ts`, step 6     | each poster's `h3_res9` with its scan count, floored per poster-hour |
| SharedReport corridor map       | the same file, step 7 (`scan_cell_aggregates`) | res-9 cells with their scan counts; cells under K are dropped        |

Exports are already res-7 only (`src/lib/export-geography.ts`, `assertCorridorGrainRow`). A sparse
**res-7** cell has the same problem one level up. A rural corridor cell is about 5.2 km², and in
corridor returns or the corridor baseline, five people there may be most of a hamlet.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **The density input is exogenous.** The rule that sets resolution reads a static table built from
   the US Census American Community Survey (ACS): block-group population divided by land area. The
   table is keyed by res-7 cell and stamped with its ACS vintage. It is generated by a script and
   checked into the repo. A new vintage arrives only by PR, the same way
   `src/lib/zip-areas-data.ts` (Census ZCTA centers, generated by `scripts/gen-zip-areas.mjs`)
   already does it. Nothing a participant does can move the table.
2. **Option 2 (resolution coarsening) is the mechanism the owner leans toward, not yet chosen.** A
   value whose cell falls in a sparse density class is published at a coarser cell, never at its own:
   - a res-9 under the first threshold publishes as its res-7 parent;
   - a res-7 under the second threshold publishes as its res-6 parent, or is withheld (open question 3).

   K stays 5 everywhere, and the count rules in `count-floor.ts` do not change. The change sits in the
   geography layer, which is where ADR-011 already decides resolution.

3. **It fails closed.** A cell missing from the table counts as sparse and is coarsened. A table that
   fails to load stops publication at res-9 and res-7. It never falls back to the fine resolution.
4. **The report says so.** A coarsened figure carries a plain line on the report, worded by the owner,
   along these lines: "Reported at the resolution that keeps your neighbours anonymous." A sponsor in
   Sawyer gets coarser figures than a sponsor in Mount Greenwood, and the report tells them why. That
   trade is a product decision, and the owner has said it is worth making openly.
5. **The public privacy statement (OSE-1590) says that density affects resolution.**

## Alternatives considered

### Option 1: density-adaptive K

Raise the floor where the population is small.

**Not preferred because:** it creates a second K with its own wording ("fewer than 12" in one place
and "fewer than 5" in another), touches every caller of `count-floor.ts`, and leaves the fine cell on
the page. Coarsening keeps one K and one wording, and moves the adaptation into the layer that already
owns resolution.

### Option 3: withhold rather than publish in sparse cells

**Not preferred on its own because:** rural corridors would see their own figures vanish. It is kept
as the bottom step of option 2 (open question 3).

### Rejected: a threshold derived from our own data

A moving average of density across the cells in play, applied back to the data, fails three ways.

1. A parameter computed from the data is itself a statistic about it, so publishing under it leaks the
   distribution that set it.
2. It breaks monotonicity in time. As data arrives the average moves, and a figure can switch between
   published and withheld with no change in its own cell.
3. **It couples cells, which turns a local attack into a remote one.** If what is publishable about
   cell A depends on data in cell B, an attacker who injects scans in B changes what is published
   about A without going near it. That is worse than the organizer probe (OSE-1780), because it no
   longer needs physical access. This alone disqualifies it.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The protection the floor gives stops depending on where an event happens.
- No feedback loop: the parameter is a fixed artifact that can be audited and reviewed in a PR.
- It also blunts the injection attack in OSE-1946's analysis (`.claude/outputs/OSE-1946-ANALYTIC-LAYER-QUERY-BOUND-2026-11-10.md`, PR #1166), which works best where real churn is lowest, in sparse cells.

### Negative

- Rural sponsors and organizers get coarser maps and coarser poster figures. "Corridor" means a larger
  area in a sparse place.
- A static table goes stale between ACS vintages, and new housing is not reflected until the next
  vintage.
- One table more to generate and size. Storing only the cells above the lowest threshold keeps it
  small, and the fail-closed default covers every cell left out.

### Neutral

- ACS is US-only. Outside the US there is no table, so every cell is treated as sparse until a source
  is chosen.

## Open questions (none can be guessed)

1. **The mechanism:** option 2, as the owner leans, or another. Owner.
2. **The thresholds:** which ACS density sends a res-9 to res-7, and which sends a res-7 to res-6 or
   to withholding. These come from the external reviewer, and this ADR must not supply numbers.
3. **The bottom step** for a sparse res-7: coarsen to res-6, or withhold. Owner.
4. **The ACS vintage and table:** for example the 2019–2023 5-year estimates; block-group population
   (B01003) over land area. To be pinned when the table is generated.
5. **Back-reporting:** whether reports already shown at res-9 are re-rendered coarser. Owner.

## Revisit criteria

- A new ACS vintage. Regenerate the table, and re-check the thresholds with the reviewer.
- Expansion outside the US.
- If OSE-1780's noise is adopted for small figures, check whether coarsening is still needed for the
  same cells, or whether the two compose.
