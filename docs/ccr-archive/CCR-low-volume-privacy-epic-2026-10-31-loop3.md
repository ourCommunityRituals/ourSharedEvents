## 3. Loop 3: Critical, Conservative, Recursive

**Critical (what is still being rationalized rather than verified?).** Four things. (1) That S11 rounding is the organizer control. It leaves the presence bit on every poster figure and leaves the event-level who-side figures exact (L3-01, L3-02). (2) That read-time withholding is "derived, nothing stored, cannot fail open" and that "a new T applies to all history". Four stores contradict it (L3-03). (3) That S1 and S11 compose. Once parts are multiples of 5, S1 cannot fire (L3-05). (4) That 45 loop-2 findings closed by edit. Fifteen did.

**Conservative (worst case: does the floor hold, and would I defend this if it shipped?).** No. Against the adversary the owner named first, an organizer who hangs a poster at one person's door: injecting 4 scans tells them, per hour, whether that person scanned (L3-02); with the live poll and no S4 (the proposed core cut) one scan at a time gives the exact r (L3-07); on a one-poster event, `sample_size` and `this_event_count` give k + r exactly with no rounding at all (L3-01). Against outsiders, the new quarantine lets one address remove a whole event-hour from the who-side figures in about 17 s (L3-08), and stored rows written before a flood stay published (L3-03). I would not defend this set if it shipped.

**Recursive (score, against loops 1 and 2, and the confidence of loop 2).** The score has held at 0% across three loops without inflation; the open deduction behind it grew (181, 476.5, 673). Per the confidence decay rule the process does not advance. Loop 2's 85% held up: every load-bearing loop-2 claim re-read still stands, except the gating half of L2-24, which v3 refuted correctly (G-17, verified by the requesting lead). Loop 2 also bred loop-3 findings: its section 12 proposed the who-side quarantine (now L3-03, L3-08, L3-09) and its remediation item A6 proposed the quiet-drain wait that the SDET shows is vacuous (L3-30). ## 10. The design finding: what the controls do against an organizer who chooses the location

**Setting.** The organizer hangs a poster at one person's door. In each hour, r is the number of real scans; at a door r is usually 0 or 1, so "r >= 1" is "the target scanned this hour". The organizer can send k scans of its own at any time. Published figure = withheld if r + k < 5, else (with S11) the largest multiple of 5 not above r + k. Flooring then rounding equals rounding then flooring (V9).

| k \ r | 0   | 1   | 2   | 3   | 4   | What the organizer learns              |
| ----- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | -------------------------------------- |
| 4     | W   | 5   | 5   | 5   | 5   | r >= 1 (presence)                      |
| 5     | 5   | 5   | 5   | 5   | 5   | nothing below 5 (only r >= 5 at r = 5) |
| 9     | 5   | 10  | 10  | 10  | 10  | r >= 1 (presence)                      |
| 10    | 10  | 10  | 10  | 10  | 10  | nothing below 5                        |

In general k leaks "r >= 5 - (k mod 5)"; k = 4 or 9 gives the presence bit. Adaptive (live poll, no sealing): send 1, 2, 3, 4 in turn; the figure first reads 5 at k = 5 - r, so r is exact. Across hours with a stable r, choosing k = 1 to 4 in four hours also yields r. Without rounding (today), any k >= 5 - r gives r exactly.

**What each control does and does not do:**

- **Rounding (S11):** removes the exact r for a fixed, non-adaptive k. Does not remove the presence bit. Does not touch `sample_size`, `this_event_count`, baselines or rates, which publish k + r exactly on a one-poster event (L3-01). Costs every sold figure up to 4.
- **Sealed hours (S4):** one read per sealed hour; removes the adaptive in-hour attack and the minute of the scan. Does not remove the per-hour presence bit, delivered about 1 h 15 min late. Costs short events their live figure.
- **Volume rule (S9a, T = 100):** irrelevant to the probe (4 is far below T). Adds a frame-a-rival lever.
- **Quarantine (S9b):** irrelevant to the probe; cannot un-publish stored rows (L3-03); quadratic (L3-09); hands one address a who-side denial (L3-08).
- **Any deterministic threshold** (a higher floor, an event-level gate, a minimum placement count) leaks the same bit at k = threshold - 1. This is the general result: no deterministic rule bounds it.

**Options that bound it, with costs:**

1. **Noise (D5).** Per-cell seeded noise or randomized rounding with a stable seed per (cell, hour) so repeated reads do not average out; totals as sums of noised parts. Gives a provable bound on what any injector learns (not zero). Costs: figures above 5 are no longer exact; a sold-metric change; its own design, prototype and CCR; RA-1 review trigger.
2. **Placement control or review.** Posters only at verified public venues, or poster-level figures withheld for placements that are not venues. Costs: friction; "place, not person" means the platform cannot know who frequents a place; a café beside the target still works. Raises cost, does not bound.
3. **Unforgeable scans.** Count a scan only with a second touch (a signed cookie issued on the event page load), a rotating on-display code, or a presence window. Costs: JavaScript and a cookie on the event page (invariant 4 scope), undercount, does not fit static posters; an organizer with four real phones still injects. Raises cost, does not bound.
4. **Accept the presence bit** as a written residual (RA-1 extended to the organizer-chooses-the-location variant, with the presence wording). Not a bound; contradicts the first goal as the owner stated it.

**What v3 must change:** say the presence bit plainly in 0.5, E-4 and H; extend any rounding to `sample_size`, `this_event_count`, `density_ratio`, baselines, rates, `headcount_trend` and exports, or withhold them at mid and premium; keep S11 and S4 together or drop both; replace S9b (it cannot un-publish stored rows) or drop it; resolve S1 against S11 by the Data Scientist's recommendation: derive D from the published poster parts (D - P = 0 by construction) and treat `sample_size` and `this_event_count` the same way; parametrize S11 AC1 over k = 1 to 9 and r = 0 to 4 with the table above as the expected result.
