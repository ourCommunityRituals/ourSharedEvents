## 3. Loop 2: Critical, Conservative, Recursive

**Critical (what did we assume solved that we never verified?).** Three things v2 treated as settled are not. (a) "O1 and O2 close padding" (v2:77). Signing routes any forged or missing cookie to a fresh id under the cap (E4, v2:43), so a cookie-less client still pads to the cap; signing adds nothing on that path. (b) "No `REQUIRED_DB_MIGRATION` bump" (v2:84, 246, 449). `check-schema-guard.mjs` fails T0 the moment a `0105` file exists. (c) "The design stands or falls on a test" (v2:220). Neither the miniflare test nor the paced staging step can exercise concurrent writers, so the gate cannot fail. Underneath all three sits the most dangerous assumption carried from loop 1: that RA-1 accepted the real probe. It did not (section 8).

**Conservative (worst case: does the floor hold?).** It does not, in three independent ways. (1) Exact-count probe: anyone who can send scans adds 5 cookie-less scans and reads 5 + r, learning the exact r in 0 to 4 (V1). (2) Denial: one address at the edge limit fills the 100 cap in about 17 s; every real scan after that is dropped and the cell reads a fabricated "100 or more" (V2). The cap converts inflation into control of the cell. (3) Existing code, missed by loop 1: event-hour `distinct_scanners` minus floored `poster_attribution` can leave the exact size of a withheld group of 1 to 4 (L2-09). Invariant 2 says the floor binds a brand's own admin; all three are available to one.

**Recursive (is loop 1's confidence still defensible?).** The loop-1 score (0%) stands; its confidence (80%) was slightly low, not high: loop 2 confirmed every loop-1 finding it re-read. What loop 1 got wrong was narrower: its risk record. The Conservative paragraph of loop 1 already said a padded cell "reads exactly as 5 plus the real scans" (worksheet:27), but RA-1 recorded only "whether at least one real scan occurred" (worksheet:95). Loop 2 is systemic, not isolated: the capped state, the withheld wording and the reader inventories each fail in three or more places, so per protocol 2.1 no partial fix should be attempted before the full surface is scoped.

## 12. Data poisoning (added 2026-10-31 by the lead at the owner's request; NOT yet reviewed by the team)

The owner's standing posture (memory, 2026-09-15, agentic-swarm threat model) names the prize as re-identification or poisoned aggregates, assumes machine-speed, rotating, sacrificial attackers ("per-IP rate limiting alone is theater"), and says suppression "must hold even under injected volume". Loops 1 and 2 scored injection mainly as a privacy probe and as inflation; this section treats poisoning as its own threat. Basis: source-read (ledger lines, `beacon-token.ts` header, `forms.ts` limiter comments, `shared-report.ts` baseline code); nothing proven at runtime.

**Who can poison, and what they gain**

| Actor                      | Surface                                                                                                                                            | Effect                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Outside swarm              | `/q` scans (forged or fresh cookies), `/s` taps, share opens, forms (register, check-in, survey), sponsor beacons                                  | Inflate counts the sponsor and organizer rely on; push a cell past the floor to read it (the exact-count probe); fill a cap to stop real counting (denial) |
| Organizer or event manager | Their own poster, link, forms; admin writes                                                                                                        | Inflate their own numbers; change a printed `/s` target with no audit row (finding 14)                                                                     |
| Sponsor                    | Clicks and display beacons for its own placement                                                                                                   | Inflate clicks; a "backroom" click arrangement                                                                                                             |
| Any of them, downstream    | Distinct counts, `return_rate`, corridor baselines (30-day rolling, `CORRIDOR_BASELINE_MIN_SAMPLE = 10` in `shared-report.ts:85`), BigQuery export | Ten forged ids satisfy the minimum sample; a baseline or return rate drifts and the corrupted figure flows into the sellable BI and the export             |

**What already exists:** link-preview and no-User-Agent refusal on `/s` and share opens (ledger entries around lines 1546 and 1613); sponsor beacons need an HMAC token and pass an invalid-traffic filter (`beacon-token.ts`, `invalid-traffic.ts`); a per-IP limiter (60/min) on form POSTs; per-ip:event limits on register and email sign-up.

**What does not hold, in the code read this turn**

- `/q` has no robot rule, and a forged or absent cookie counts as a new person (loop 1, verified).
- The sponsor token is minted into every public sponsor-bundle response (`beacon-token.ts` header: "anyone who reads a public sponsor-bundle response can see a valid pair"), so a scripted client can harvest tokens; the short TTL limits reuse, not harvesting.
- The ledger records that forms have no per-event cap: "nothing bounds submissions to one event_id spread across many IPs, so checkin_counts and form_surveys can be inflated by a sender rotating IPs" (ledger line 1200).
- Signing does not prevent harvesting: a cookie-less request returns a fresh signed id, so free valid ids are limited only by the per-IP request rate (NF-7).
- The cap as drafted turns inflation into denial and publishes an attacker-chosen "N or more" (NF-2).

**How this changes the plan (proposed, for loop 3 review)**

1. **Replace "drop at the cap" with "withhold on anomaly".** Keep storing every scan. When a poster-hour is implausible (velocity or total above a rule), publish nothing for that cell and exclude it from sums, instead of publishing an attacker-set number. Failure mode becomes withholding, which is the safe side for the first goal (no risk of identification). Cost: an attacker can force withholding (availability, not value). Open: the anomaly signal needs sub-hour timing, which the platform promises not to store at rest; a short-TTL counter that sets one boolean per poster-hour is the candidate, and it needs the Dr of Security and Data Scientist to review it.
2. **Baselines, returns and exports ignore unverified input:** only validly signed ids count toward distinct, return and baseline figures, and quarantined hours are excluded; the BigQuery export carries a provenance flag so poisoned hours cannot enter the BI silently.
3. **Detection:** persist the invalid-traffic reason as a private counter (not the sponsor-facing share that slice 6 proposed) so poisoning can be seen at all; add an alert (finding 21).
4. **Forms:** a per-event bound on check-ins, registrations and surveys (ledger 1200), and the live check-in tile crossing the floor live (parked ticket E) comes back into scope.
5. **Sponsor and organizer collusion:** audit rows for placement, deployment and short-link writes (finding 14) carry more weight; the invalid-traffic reason counter is the only evidence of inflation by the actors who benefit.
6. **RA-1 versus the standing posture:** RA-1 accepts that an injector learns an exact small count. The owner's own posture says suppression must hold under injected volume. The owner should decide knowingly whether RA-1 is a deliberate exception to that posture, or whether D5 (noise) or a stronger anomaly rule replaces it.

**Design consequences (proposed)**

1. Treat the organizer as an adversary of attendees in the threat model, not only outsiders. This sharpens RA-1: the exact-count probe is cheapest for the party who picks the poster location.
2. Withhold-on-anomaly must be reviewed for frame-a-rival abuse; a private reason counter should be visible to the platform, not published beside a named sponsor.
3. Make colluders leave evidence: audit rows on placement, deployment and short-link writes (finding 14); cross-source consistency (scan to check-in ratio, scan to display ratio) as a private anomaly signal; one organizer's share of a sponsor's reach as a concentration signal.
4. Say what a count claims: "counted scans", not verified presence; the report states what is counted versus corroborated.
5. Keep poisoned cells out of baselines, returns and the export (quarantine flag), so the shared data asset is not contaminated by a colluding trio.
6. Presence-bound scanning for the live window (a code that rotates on the display, or a window check) is an option for events that need stronger evidence; it does not fit static printed posters.

**Owner decision (2026-10-31): organizers are in scope as adversaries of attendees**, alongside outsiders; sponsors and attendee collusion likewise. The first goal is no risk of identification; poisoning is treated as its own threat (section 12).

**12c. What this decision changes (proposed for loop 3; nothing built)**

- **RA-1 is not extended automatically.** The reworded RA-1 covers "anyone who can send scans to a poster and read the figure, a brand admin included". It does not say that the organizer chooses where the poster goes. Treat the organizer-picks-the-location variant as not accepted until the owner confirms it or a control below is chosen.
- **Controls to evaluate, with cost:**
  - **A. Placement review.** Posters only at registered venues or public-entity-verified places. Friction, and "place, not person" means the platform cannot verify who frequents a place.
  - **B. Event-level gate for poster-level figures.** A poster-level number is released only once the event total, across several placements, clears a higher threshold. Raises the attacker's cost; still injectable.
  - **C. Round and delay.** Poster-level figures only after the event closes, rounded down to multiples of 5 as exports already are. This hides the exact r above the floor, but a threshold still shows whether at least one real scan occurred.
  - **D. Noise on small published counts (D5).** The only option that gives a provable bound. It ends the promise that a number at 5 or more is exact.
  - **E. Attribution and deterrence.** Organizers are authenticated, so audit rows on placement creation and edits, plus a private anomaly signal, make a targeting attempt traceable. Deterrence, not prevention: an organizer's scans arrive through the public route and look like anyone's.
- **Likely combination to review:** C plus E now; D if the owner wants a bound rather than deterrence; A only if the product can verify venues.
